Lenses — read before examining a question a second way

A lens is one way of examining a question: a method, a body of evidence, or a stake in the outcome. A second lens is a way that could reach a different answer where the first could not.

**If one careful pass answers the question, say so in one sentence and do the pass. Most questions are this.**

A second pass is not a second lens. A lens earns the name only if it could have failed where the first one succeeded: different evidence, a different method, or a different thing at stake, never a different vocabulary over the same reasoning. Test each candidate by naming a case it gets wrong; two lenses that get the same cases wrong are one lens.

**If you are one agent producing all the answers, you cannot form them independently. Say so, and weight the agreement as one voice.** Count independent methods, not voices: correlated panelists vote once however many seats they fill, and three restatements are one claim counted three times.

Required output, when you do convene:
1. **Irreducible differences first.** Where the lenses disagree, and why that disagreement is structural rather than one of them being wrong, is the finding. Lead with it.
2. **The lens you would not convene again.** Name one lens you convened that you would not convene again on this question, what you expected it to see, and what it saw instead. Discarding a *claim* everyone had already dropped does not satisfy this; the thing discarded must be a lens.
3. **A minority slot.** The strongest surviving non-consensus position, named, with what would settle it. "None" is a valid entry; dropping it silently is not. Aggregation is where correct minority answers die.
4. **Refuse synthesis when integrating would cost the finding.** Say the lenses do not reconcile, and what each is right about. Do not average, do not split the difference, do not mechanically choose the midpoint. Some contradictions are the deliverable.
